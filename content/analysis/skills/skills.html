Skills Required for Success

Engineering. R is my home language; SQL and relational data are daily work. I build research infrastructure and repeatable reports, not just one-off analyses. I’m familiar with Python, but wouldn’t claim the same depth.

AI in my own work. I use it to help engineer data, explore it, and publish the results. My agent harness organizes that collaboration; Absent from Chronic shows it at work. The question for me is how to make an analyst’s work more useful and inspectable, not simply generate more code.

Methods. My depth is in regression, longitudinal modelling, and studying populations. I’ve led employment-service evaluation, developed forecasting comparisons, and used LASSO for variable selection. I want to understand the question, test the assumptions, and make uncertainty visible.

Working together. I can carry a study from question to report, but independent work needn’t be solitary. I build with colleagues, teach and mentor, and make tools others can reuse. Listening to the people who know the service matters as much as explaining the model. Shared examples give us something concrete to discuss, question, and improve.

Room to grow. I don’t claim equal depth across your whole ML stack. My agent work isn’t evidence of production RAG or self-hosted inference experience; the appendix makes those boundaries explicit. I’m happy to pick up more tech as the work calls for it, starting with a small, testable problem.

Public service. Your commitments fit how I want to work: be helpful, accountable, and willing to learn. For equity, diversity, reconciliation, and inclusion, I’d start by listening, questioning whose experience the data misses, and learning from colleagues and communities rather than assuming expertise.
